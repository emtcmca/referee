    Abstract232 cut

    plit-window brightness temperatures at Erewhon Station have been reported by four successive radiometer generations since 1997, and the published series has never been reconciled across the three instrument changeovers. We specify a replication protocol that reprocesses all four archives through a single retrieval chain, holding the coefficient set fixed and varying only the instrument response function. Applied to 11,412 clear-sky scenes, the protocol reduces the inter-generation offset from 0.94 K to 0.11 K and isolates a 0.31 K step at the 2009 changeover that the original calibration notes do not record. We release the reprocessing chain, the coefficient tables, and the scene index, so that any of the four archives can be regenerated from raw counts by a reader with no access to the instruments.

    MethodsF-000004
    quoted

    Each archive was decoded from raw counts using the vendor response function published for that generation, then passed through an identical retrieval chain. Coefficients were fit once, on the 1997–2001 overlap window, and held fixed for every later generation; no per-generation tuning was permitted at any stage. Scenes were screened for cloud using the Zemblan two-channel test at a 0.6 threshold, and the 11,412 surviving scenes are indexed by acquisition time rather than by orbit, because two of the four archives do not record orbit number. We report the inter-generation offset as the median difference over co-located scene pairs within 90 seconds and 4 km, which discards 8 percent of otherwise usable pairs but removes the parallax term that dominated earlier comparisons.

    DiscussionF-000008
    quoted251 cut

    The residual 0.11 K offset is small enough that the four archives can now be treated as one series for trend work, but we caution against reading the 2009 step as an instrument artifact alone. The changeover coincided with a relocation of the radiometer mast by 11 m, and we cannot separate the two effects with the scenes available. A reader who needs that separation will need the pre-2009 raw counts, which Erewhon Station has not released and which we were not able to obtain. We regard this as the principal limitation of the protocol, and not as a matter that further processing can resolve.

    Two reviewers of an earlier draft asked whether the protocol generalizes to the Vespucian fog-belt instruments. We think it does not, without modification: those instruments report at 1 Hz rather than per scene, and the co-location rule above has no meaning at that cadence. Adapting the chain would require a resampling step that we have deliberately left out here, because it introduces a free parameter and the point of the protocol is that it has none.

    What this does not solve

    honesty boundary · stated in full, in the app, in the README and in the description

    Referee’s injection detector is a small set of pattern families tuned against fixtures we wrote ourselves. It catches the payloads in this corpus and a determined author could evade it in an afternoon. Prompt injection is not solved here and we make no claim that it is. The architectural claim is narrower and does not depend on the detector: the page does not promise the agent clean text, it promises a declared boundary with a known location. Both tools that return author-derived text carry the WebMCP standard’s own untrustedContentHint, which stays true no matter how good or bad our detection is; author identity is absent from every tool return rather than filtered out of it; a finding is refused unless its evidence quote verifies against the text the agent was actually given; and the final recommendation is not a tool the agent can call. If the detector misses a payload, the agent can still be argued into a bad review, and it still cannot learn who wrote the paper, cite text that is not there, or decide the outcome.

    About Referee

    What this is

    A double-blind review room where a human reviewer and a browser-resident assistant work the same queue of twelve manuscripts. The assistant reads through seven tools registered on the page. The page, not a system prompt, decides what those tools return.

    When a page mediates between an agent and untrusted content, it can enforce things the agent cannot enforce for itself: what it may see, what it may claim, and what it may decide.

    The seven stops

    Every tool return hands the assistant a next_expected_action with a plain-English reason. The numbered marks on the page are the same field, turned toward the reviewer. Ignore them and the room still works; follow them and this is the order.

    1. 1Start here. Twelve manuscripts, ranked, with a visible accept cut.
    2. 2The one you opened. One manuscript fills the reading column.
    3. 3What it may see. Nine identity fields, absent from every return.
    4. 4What it may claim. A finding refused because no quote carried it.
    5. 5What was cut out. Two hidden instructions, removed before the return was built.
    6. 6What counts. Move a weight and watch the slate re-rank.
    7. 7What only you may decide. Commit, and the queue moves on.

    The corpus is fictional

    All twelve manuscripts, their authors, institutions, instruments, datasets and results were written for this demo. Every proper noun is drawn from a fixed literary-fictional list — Erewhon, Zembla, Laputa, Vespucia, Grand Fenwick, Ruritania. Emails and links use the reserved .invalid suffix; identifiers are ORCID-shaped but come from the all-zero reserved block and the field is named orcid_like. Nothing here is a real study, dataset, institution, or person.

    Honesty boundary

    Referee’s injection detector is a small set of pattern families tuned against fixtures we wrote ourselves. It catches the payloads in this corpus and a determined author could evade it in an afternoon. Prompt injection is not solved here and we make no claim that it is. The architectural claim is narrower and does not depend on the detector: the page does not promise the agent clean text, it promises a declared boundary with a known location. Both tools that return author-derived text carry the WebMCP standard’s own untrustedContentHint, which stays true no matter how good or bad our detection is; author identity is absent from every tool return rather than filtered out of it; a finding is refused unless its evidence quote verifies against the text the agent was actually given; and the final recommendation is not a tool the agent can call. If the detector misses a payload, the agent can still be argued into a bad review, and it still cannot learn who wrote the paper, cite text that is not there, or decide the outcome.

    How it was built

    Vanilla ES modules. No bundler, no framework, no backend, no network calls, and no model calls from the page. One committed light mode, chosen rather than omitted: a theme toggle would double the contrast-verification surface for nothing a reader can see.
